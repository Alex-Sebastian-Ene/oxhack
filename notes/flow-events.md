A PortWatch dip is not a lost barrel: a scenario fed from live AIS has to tell a closure from a winter dip.

- **Power.** Brent's own 90% range over 20 trading days is -18% to +17% (2019–2025). At the 2026 response (0.64% per point at +20) a loss below 26 Hormuz points cannot be told from noise; at +120 (0.27% per point) not even the 2026 closure can (178 points would be needed). No event since 2019 other than the closure is big enough to test the elasticity's size; the out-of-sample events test its sign and the role of rerouting.
- **The 2026 closure after +120 days.** Brent data now runs 159 trading days past day 0. Brent per point of strait flow lost was lowest in trading days 64–84 (0.16%), when the bypass ports made up 41% of the Gulf's lost exports, and highest in days 148–159 (0.70%), when they made up none: their own exports had fallen below the pre-closure level (-12% of the Gulf's loss). - **Proposed curve** (`notes/flow-premium.csv`, log premium per point of lost Hormuz flow): a jump of 0.28% per point on day 1, rising to 0.75% on day 22, decaying with a 9-day half-life to a plateau of 0.39%, fitted to all 159 observed days; 90% band from the placebo (one event's noise). Beyond day 159 it is an assumption: flat at the plateau, the band still widening.

## The rule, fixed before looking at Brent

Set after looking at the PortWatch flow series and before computing any Brent change around an event:

- flow: the 7-day mean of daily tanker capacity as a share of the chokepoint's 2019–2024 average
- an event starts on the first day the 7-day share is at least 25 points below its pre-level (mean over days −29..−7) and stays there for 14 days running (`flow_price.SUSTAIN`); one event per chokepoint per 182 days
- searched 2019-02-01 to 2025-12-31 at Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb; an event is out of sample if its +120-day window ends before 2026-02-01
- news-matched: a dated disruption at that chokepoint between 45 days before and 14 days after day 0
- lost flow at +h: the pre-level minus the mean share from day 0 to +h; Brent: log change from 5 trading days before day 0
- placebo: the same Brent change from all 1207 other trading days of 2019–2025 more than 20 trading days from an event or a dated disruption, windows ending before 2026-02-01; the band is its 5%–95% range divided by the event's lost flow

## Rerouted, bypassed and lost barrels

A tanker that goes round the Cape instead of through the Red Sea still delivers its cargo, two to three weeks later; only the barrels that do not show up anywhere are lost to the market. At Bab el-Mandeb the Cape's gain gives the rerouted part. Hormuz has no sea route around it: the only way round is by pipeline, the Saudi East–West line to Yanbu on the Red Sea and the UAE's Habshan–Fujairah line to the Gulf of Oman. PortWatch's tanker exports at those two ports, against exports from seven ports inside the Gulf (macro_data's `pipeline` block), show how much of the Gulf's lost exports the pipelines made up in 2026.

Over all 159 days the bypass ports made up 13% of the Gulf ports' lost exports. What this means for the Hormuz elasticity:

- The chain's `lost` is points of tanker flow through the strait, and its elasticity has the 2026 bypass built in. A point of strait flow lost while the pipelines run full is a smaller net loss than a point lost while they are down; the 2026 path shows both, and Brent per gross point was lowest while the bypass carried the most and highest once it had failed. Per net point the swing is smaller but does not vanish (the strait also partly reopened in the low block: its 7-day share reached 37%).
- Bab el-Mandeb's barrels were mostly rerouted, and its Brent response per Hormuz-equivalent point was below the 2026 closure's at +20 days (and close to it at +120 days), inside noise either way. That fits a market that prices lost barrels, not rerouted ones, but one event this small cannot show it.
- So for a Hormuz scenario the elasticity should be read per point of strait flow with the pipelines responding as in 2026. A scenario with the pipelines out of action (as from late July 2026) belongs in the upper half of the band; one where the pipelines carry their full capacity from the first day belongs in the lower half.

How it is built, and what it assumes:

- **Mid, days 1–159:** a least-squares fit to the 2026 closure's daily path (Brent's log change from 5 days before day 0, divided by the 82 points lost, the chain's `LOST_2026`): a jump on day 1, a linear rise to the peak, an exponential decay to a plateau. Fitted: jump 0.28%, peak 0.75% on day 22, half-life 9 days, plateau 0.39% per point; the daily path strays from it by 0.19% per point (RMS); its largest swings are the dip around day 80 and the rise after day 120. The jump is there because Brent was already +14% on day 0 and +23% on day 1: the 7-day flow share was 117% five trading days before day 0 and 29% on it, so the closure began before the rule's day 0.
- **Band, days 1–159:** the mid plus the placebo's centred 5% and 95% Brent moves at that horizon (2019–2025, 1106 days), divided by the 82 points. That is the error of an elasticity read off one event: Brent would have moved that much without the closure. The low edge is floored at zero (no disruption here lowered Brent beyond noise). The band is wide because one event is all there is: from about day 142 on the low edge is zero. 92% of the 2026 path's observed days lie inside it (in sample).
- **Beyond day 159 (assumption, no data):** the mid stays at its day-159 value, 0.39% per point (the plateau), which supposes a loss that lasts keeps its premium: no further fading from demand cuts, stock releases or new supply, and no further rise from running stocks down. The 2026 path between days 60 and the last observed day did not fade (it rose as the bypass failed), which is the reason for not decaying it. The band keeps widening with the placebo's longer horizons, up to 12 months.
- **The out-of-sample events do not move it.** Bab el-Mandeb's response is inside the noise of the 2026 response scaled to its size, and the other dips are not supply losses, so there is nothing to pool with the 2026 closure; they are consistent with its sign and with rerouted barrels counting for less.
- **Against the chain now:** the chain's peak is 0.63% per point on day 20 (the highest close within 90 days) and its level after day 120 is 0.24% (one day's close). The fit's plateau, 0.39%, uses every observed day after the peak, including the rise after day 120.
- **Use in the chain:** the band is the uncertainty of the mean premium, not of Brent's path; the GARCH paths add Brent's own noise on top. Draw one curve per path (for example e_mid plus a standard normal times the half-width on its side over 1.645, clipped at zero) so the two are not merged into one number.

**Limits.** Every number past the 2026 closure itself comes from events too small to test the size of the elasticity: the out-of-sample evidence checks the sign and the rerouting argument, not the scale. The placebo is 2019–2025, which includes the COVID collapse and 2022; a calmer period would give a narrower band. PortWatch counts AIS-visible tankers: its Hormuz share dips in several winters with no known disruption, and it misses dark-fleet transits. Capacity is deadweight tonnes of tankers passing, laden or not, so the Hormuz-equivalent scaling and the Cape's rerouted share are rough. The bypass shares use seven Gulf ports and two bypass ports, not every terminal.
