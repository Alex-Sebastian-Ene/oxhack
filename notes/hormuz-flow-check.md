## Summary

- **The IEA and PortWatch agree on the spring and part from June.** In March–May the IEA puts flows through the strait at 2.27, 3.33, 3.00 mb/d against 20.66 in February: 89, 84, 85 points of pre-war flow lost, against PortWatch's 81, 78, 79 (its share 1.1%, 3.3%, 3.2% of the 2019–2024 normal, from a pre-level of 82%). From June the IEA has the strait carrying 7.2–10.9 mb/d (47–65 points lost) while PortWatch shows about 12% of normal in June and July and under 1% from August (81 points lost). In September the IEA has the strait at 53% of its February flow (10.9 mb/d); PortWatch has it at 0.7% of normal.
- **The gap is barrels AIS cannot see.** The IEA counts oil that leaves the Gulf through the strait whether or not its transponder is on, including dark exits along the Omani coast and Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship transfers; PortWatch counts AIS-visible tankers crossing its strait polygon. Lloyd's List counted at least 112 non-Iranian-linked crude tanker transits in August; PortWatch logged 58 tanker transits of every kind, in both directions. PortWatch's pre-war level is low too: its February share, 84%, is the winter dip it shows every year, while the IEA has February at 103% of the 2025 average. So the IEA series is the better measure of barrels lost.
- **Refitted per point of the IEA's lost flow, day by day:** a jump of 0.25% per point on day 1, rising to 0.70% on day 22, decaying with a 4-day half-life to a plateau of 0.51% (now: 0.28%, 0.75% on day 22, 9 days, 0.39%). The first two months move little (-5% on average over days 1–42: the IEA's loss is 89, 84, 85 points, a little more than the trough's 82). After them the curve is +28% higher on average over the observed days, and the level a scenario keeps beyond the data is 0.51% per point against 0.39%.
- **But per IEA point, 2026 does not follow one decaying curve.** Brent per point lost was 0.60% in April, 0.29% in June–July and 1.08% in September–October, when the IEA has the strait recovering. That autumn rise is not a strait-flow effect: the East–West pipeline was bombed on 11 September, Houthi attacks cut the Red Sea bypass, and stocks were drawing at record rates. Left free to rise, the fit chases it and gives up April (its day-159 level would be 0.74%); the recommended fit keeps the decay the curve is meant to have. Either way it fits Brent's daily move a little worse in sample than the trough (RMS 17.3% against 15.4%), and neither gets September–October.
- **Bab el-Mandeb still checks out:** the 2026 response per point of the IEA's loss, scaled to the 2023–24 disruption, predicts its Brent move within noise at every horizon from 5 to 120 trading days, as the trough-scaled response does. The check is weak: Brent's own noise is wider than either prediction, so it cannot choose between them.
- **Recommendation: switch to the IEA's lost flow, as one change** (curve, replay path, app preset together; details at the end). A sustained 82-point loss then carries 42% of log premium after 12 months, not 32%. `notes/flow-premium.csv` stays as it is until that change lands.

## What the IEA figures count

- **Strait flow** is Gulf producers' oil exports shipped through the strait: crude, natural gas liquids and refined products (the April report's "loadings of crude, natural gas liquids and refined products"), Iran included. Pre-war, about 20 mb/d (15 crude, 5 products in 2025).
- **Ship-to-ship transfers and dark transits are inside it.** The 22 June commentary's chart has a separate "Gulf of Oman STS" column ("ship-to-ship transfers including dark activity exit of Hormuz via the Omani coastline"), and its text's 2.7 mb/d for March–May is the strait column plus that one (2.72), not the strait column alone (2.35). The 18 September commentary's chart has no STS column and its strait values for March–May are those sums revised up a little (2.27, 3.33, 3.00); the October report says outright that STS transfers lifted "total oil flows through the Strait". Our reading: from 18 September on, the IEA's strait flow includes them.
- **The bypass is separate:** Saudi Red Sea ports (the East–West pipeline to Yanbu), the UAE's Habshan–Fujairah pipeline, Iraq via Türkiye and Syria, and Iran's Jask terminal.
- **"Total flows" in the June report is strait plus bypass:** its May low of 9.6 mb/d is the 22 June chart's three columns summed (9.63), and the July report's June total (16.1 mb/d, "including volumes bypassing the Strait") is 6.5 above it. It is not a strait figure.
- **Revisions.** August's strait flow was 7.58 mb/d (preliminary) on 18 September and 7.2 in the October report (September's 10.9 less its 3.7 rise); March–May rose by 0.03–0.23 mb/d between the June and September charts. The fit uses the latest figure for each month.

## Why they differ

- **Dark transits.** "Since April, the UAE has also been ramping up exports through the Strait of Hormuz along the Omani coastline with tankers' transponders turned off to avoid detection" (IEA commentary, 22 June). A tanker with AIS off is not in PortWatch's count. Lloyd's List, which adds transits it identifies as dark, counted at least 112 non-Iranian-linked crude tanker transits in August ([Lloyd's List Intelligence, Strait of Hormuz Brief, 9 September 2026](https://www.lloydslistintelligence.com/resources/blog/strait-of-hormuz-brief-9-september-2026)), and expects the figure to rise as more dark transits are found; PortWatch has 58 tanker transits of all types for the month.
- **Ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman.** Oil shuttled out along the Omani coast and transferred to larger ships outside the strait is strait flow for the IEA; the shuttles are small, often dark, and the large ships never cross PortWatch's polygon. The IEA's STS column rose from 0.30 mb/d in April to 1.78 in the first half of June, and the October report credits "robust" STS for September's rise to 10.9 mb/d.
- **AIS gaps and spoofing.** PortWatch's Hormuz share dips every winter with no supply lost (61–82% of normal in Q1 from 2019 to 2025, `notes/flow-events.md`), so its 2026 pre-level is low before the war starts. Jamming and spoofed positions in the Gulf hide or misplace ships further (Windward: 61% of logged August transits false because of spoofing, via `docs/ideas.md`; not checked at source).
- **The barrels had to go somewhere.** Gulf production recovered to 23.9 mb/d in July (IEA, August report) and oil on water rose 103 mb in September "amid a sharp rise in Gulf exports" (October report). Gulf storage was filling from March (March and April reports) and the bypass carried 4.6–7.8 mb/d from June, so that output needed several mb/d through the strait; PortWatch's share under 1% (about 0.2 mb/d of a 20 mb/d normal) cannot carry it. Production and stocks are measured apart from tanker tracking, so this check does not lean on the IEA's strait estimate alone.
- **What PortWatch measures is different anyway:** deadweight tonnes of tankers passing, laden or in ballast, both ways. In normal times that tracks barrels; in 2026, with dark laden exits and a changed fleet, it does not.

## The premium curve per point of the IEA's lost flow (`notes/flow-premium-iea.csv`)

Each observed trading day's Brent move (log change from 5 trading days before day 0, as now) is divided by that day's lost flow, and the same jump-ramp-decay-plateau curve is fitted with each day weighted by its lost flow squared, which fits the move itself (move ≈ lost × curve). With a constant loss that is the current fit. The band is the same placebo divided by each day's loss, held at the last day's beyond the data. The plateau may not exceed the peak (the shape is a decay; the trough's fit is the same with or without that rule). Three other fits show what drives the change: PortWatch day by day (its 7-day share against the 82% pre-level), the IEA's mean loss to date (a premium that follows cumulative barrels lost), and the IEA fit with the tail free to rise.

| Lost flow | Lost, observed days | Jump | Peak | Peak day | Half-life | Plateau | Day-159 level | RMS error of Brent's move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PortWatch trough, held (`notes/flow-premium.csv`) | 82 | 0.28% | 0.75% | 22 | 9 days | 0.39% | 0.39% | 15.4% |
| **IEA month by month (`notes/flow-premium-iea.csv`)** | 47–89 | 0.25% | 0.70% | 22 | 4 days | 0.51% | 0.51% | 17.3% |
| PortWatch day by day | 38–82 | 0.31% | 0.76% | 22 | 9 days | 0.43% | 0.43% | 13.3% |
| IEA, mean loss to date | 67–89 | 0.26% | 0.68% | 22 | 8 days | 0.42% | 0.42% | 16.2% |

## What it does to the chain, and the recommendation

The chain's premium is `lost` × e(day). Fitted with one lost-flow measure, a curve only reproduces 2026 when it is used with that measure. In sample, by month (log premium, %):

For a scenario, the same 82 points held for 12 months give a log premium of 32% on day 262 with the current curve and 42% with the IEA's (Brent × 1.38 against × 1.52). By the IEA's count, 82 points held is a harsher closure than 2026 was: its loss averaged 67 points over the observed days and 47 in September, while Brent stood +57% (log) in October.

**Recommendation: switch to the IEA's lost flow, as one change, and keep `notes/flow-premium.csv` until then.**

- **Why switch.** The IEA's series is the better measure of barrels lost (above), and the chain's input is meant to be barrels: the app's slider is a share of normal flow, and the flow estimate that feeds the chain counts hidden ships, as the IEA does. Per point of those barrels the 2026 premium after the first two months was higher than the current curve says (+28% over days 43–159; plateau 0.51% against 0.39%). Bab el-Mandeb does not object: it stays inside the noise at every horizon.
- **What it costs.** In sample the IEA curve fits Brent's daily move a little worse (RMS 17.3% against 15.4%), and the 2026 replay's October falls from +32% to +24% against +57% actual. The trough misses less there only because it counts 81 points lost where the IEA counts 47; it does not explain the rise either. Keeping a denominator known to be wrong for a better fit in sample is not a reason to keep it.
- **Why as one change.** A curve reproduces 2026 only with the lost flow it was fitted with. With the trough the IEA curve overstates 2026 by up to 25 points of log premium (July: +42% against +16%), so the switch needs: `notes/flow-premium-iea.csv` as `notes/flow-premium.csv`; `oil_paths.premium` taking a lost-flow path by day, not one number; the 2026 replay (`macro_chain.py`, the "lost 82" scenario) and the reserves restatement (`experiments/reserves.py` on `seb/reserves`) using the IEA path (89 points in March to 47 in September); the app's 2026 preset the same; and `docs/chain.md` step 1. Until that lands, the current curve with its trough is the consistent pair.
- **What it does not fix.** Neither curve reproduces September–October: the late premium came with the bypass attacks and the stock draw, which a premium per point of strait flow does not model. The IEA curve's upper edge (e_high × lost) covers 100% of September–October's trading days; a stock-draw term would be the next step.

## Limits

- **One event still sets the size.** The IEA's barrels change the denominator, not the number of closures.
- **The IEA's figures are estimates** built from tanker tracking and other data, and they are revised: August moved from 7.58 to 7.2 mb/d between September and October, and March–May rose by 0.03–0.23 mb/d between June and September. That STS transfers sit inside the 18 September strait column is our reading of the sums, not something the chart says.
- **Monthly steps.** Each trading day takes its month's flow, so the loss changes on the first of the month; inside June the strait went from 6.3 mb/d (first half, with STS, 22 June chart) to 9.4 for the month.
- **October has no IEA figure yet** and holds September's; the October report warns that renewed attacks threaten the recovery, so the loss may have grown again.
- **Points of February's flow** (20.66 mb/d). Against the 2025 average (20 mb/d) each point is 3% smaller and the premium per point 3% larger.
- **Strait flow only.** The bypass stays inside the curve as it ran in 2026; the IEA's total exports (strait plus bypass) would be a measure of barrels lost to the market as a whole, and are in the table for that.
- **The Bab el-Mandeb conversion** into Hormuz points still uses PortWatch's tonnage at the two straits.
