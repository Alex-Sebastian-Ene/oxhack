# Emergency oil stocks: a buffer between lost Hormuz flow and Brent

_Generated by `experiments/reserves.py` on 2026-10-25. Inputs: `notes/reserves/releases-2026.csv` (the 2026 releases), `notes/reserves/stocks.csv` (stocks held for release and how fast they can go), `notes/reserves/country-cover.csv` (stocks by country) and the flow-premium curve `notes/flow-premium.csv` (generated 2026-10-24). Every row of the CSVs carries its source, link and date. Nothing here is synthetic; the model assumptions are listed under Limits._

## How the buffer works

The flow-premium curve is Brent's log premium per point of *gross* lost Hormuz flow in the 2026 closure. Part of those barrels was replaced from emergency stocks, so the curve is restated per point of *net* loss (gross minus released, in points of the pre-war 20 million barrels a day). A scenario then sets its own release: the same share of lost barrels as in 2026 (the chain as it is), none, or the IEA's maximum drawdown (up to 25 mb/d for two months, falling to nothing by six). Releases stop when the stocks held for release run out, and those barrels go missing again.

## Limits

- **The lost flow itself.** The curve's 82 points come from IMF PortWatch's AIS tanker counts, which show Hormuz near 0-2% of normal for most of the closure. The IEA puts flows through the strait at 2.7 mb/d over March-May (about 86 points lost; [commentary, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/how-global-oil-supplies-have-readjusted-to-help-fill-the-huge-gap-left-by-the-strait-of-hormuz-shock)) but 10.9 mb/d in September, helped by ship-to-ship transfers (about 45 points; [Oil Market Report, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-october-2026)). If the IEA is right, the loss shrank over the summer while the curve holds it at 82, so the late-closure premium per point is understated. That is a question for the flow estimate; it does not change how the stocks enter.
- **Proportional premium (assumption).** Brent's premium is taken to scale with the net barrels missing. One closure cannot test that: the curve still rests on 2026, and the release only reweights it.
- **Bypass pipelines** stay inside the curve as they were in 2026; they are not a separate offset here.
- **Delivery windows.** Each tranche is spread evenly over its window; actual loadings were lumpier.
- **Maximum rate and run-out.** A release at the maximum rate assumes every counted holder draws at once. In practice stocks are drawn more slowly near the bottom, and a run-out comes with a price spike this curve does not model.
- **Country cover** treats all of a country's stocks as usable for its lost Hormuz oil; the legal minimum, product mix and refinery fit all cut that in practice.
