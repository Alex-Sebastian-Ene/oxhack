_Generated by `experiments/macro_global.py report` on 2026-10-25 from 8 forward-chaining folds (2008-01..2025-10, 214 forecast origins), the same folds, training months and data as `notes/macro-bayes-results.md` (inputs: `notes/macro-panel.md`). The data are real and revised (not real-time vintages); nothing is synthetic. The forecasts in the first sections are **what-ifs**: they are given the oil path that happened. The forecast-mode sections at the end know nothing after the origin and are compared with institutional US inflation forecasts. `global`, `global_lin` in `runs/macro/global-fold-*.json` generated 2026-10-24T20:08:40+00:00 to 2026-10-24T20:16:14+00:00. `global_tcn` in `runs/macro/global-fold-*.json` generated 2026-10-24T21:22:32+00:00 to 2026-10-24T21:35:04+00:00. `runs/macro/bayes-own-fold-*.json` generated 2026-10-24T20:17:30+00:00 to 2026-10-24T20:27:01+00:00. `runs/macro/bayes-fold-*.json` generated 2026-10-24T19:59:02+00:00 to 2026-10-24T20:05:23+00:00._

Skill = 1 − CRPS / CRPS of the v1 climatology (training mean and spread), averaged over the outputs in the group; 0 is no better than climatology. `global` is one network shared by all 22 series; `global_lin` adds a per-series linear term (momentum and oil lags) to its location. `global_tcn` (added after the first two were scored) keeps that linear term but replaces the network's flat inputs with a causal TCN (16 wide, dilations 1, 2, 4, 8) over the last 24 months of the series' own change, Brent's change and the volatility state, shared by all series and predicting one month ahead like the others. `OLS, same inputs` is per-series least squares on the networks' inputs (12 own lags, oil lags 0–12, volatility state) with Gaussian errors, simulated the same way: the inputs without the network. `Bayes own-vol` is the main Bayesian model (`macro_bayes.py --vol own`: each series' own recent volatility sets its band); `Bayes oil-vol` lets the band follow oil volatility only (`--vol oil`).

## Against institutional US inflation forecasts (forecast mode)

_`runs/macro/global-benchmarks.json`: surveys fetched 2026-10-24, SPF to the 2026Q3 survey._

- **Cleveland Fed** (FRED `EXPINF1YR`, model-based 1-year expected inflation) and **Michigan** (FRED `MICH`, households' median expected price change over the next year): month t's value at the end of month t, converted to 100 × log, as in `macro_bayes.py benchmarks`. Both are 1-year rates; at shorter horizons they are scaled by h/12, like the random walk.
- **SPF**: the Philadelphia Fed's Survey of Professional Forecasters, median (and mean) headline CPI (`median_cpi_level.xlsx`, `mean_cpi_level.xlsx`). Each quarterly survey forecasts the annualised growth of the quarterly average of seasonally adjusted CPI, for its own quarter q (CPI2) and the next four (CPI3..CPI6). A survey counts as public at the end of the month of its news release, from the Philadelphia Fed's release-dates file (`spf-release-dates.txt`): mid-February, May, August or November, except 2019Q1 (released 22 March 2019 after a government shutdown). The 12-month change is read off the survey's path: each quarter's average stands for its middle month and the log level is linear in between. At the end of the middle month of quarter q (the usual release month) the 12-month change is then the survey's growth over quarters q+1..q+4 (CPI3..CPI6 de-annualised, 100 × log(1 + rate/100) / 4 each, summed), a window centred on the 12 months we score. For an origin m months later the window moves m months along the path: m/3 of quarter q+1 drops out and quarter q+4's rate carries on for m months. Shorter horizons take the first h months of the path. **Release month** scores each survey only at the end of its release month; **latest** uses the latest public survey at every month-end, 0–2 months old (3 for February 2019).
- **Real-time against revised.** The surveys were made with the data as first published; every model here is trained and scored on today's revised data, which flatters the models slightly. The target, seasonally adjusted CPI (FRED `CPIAUCSL`), is revised only through its seasonal factors.
- Point forecasts get a Gaussian band with the sd of their own errors on each fold's training origins. Skill and Diebold–Mariano tests (Newey–West, 12 lags) use the origins both forecasts share, with the v1 climatology's CRPS on those origins as the scale. *CRPS lower by* = 1 − our scaled CRPS / theirs (negative: ours is higher).
