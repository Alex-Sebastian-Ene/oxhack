# Household cost of living over time: what people pay and what they earn

_Generated by `experiments/household.py report` on 2026-10-25. US, UK and euro area, monthly from 2000. Sources: BLS via FRED, ONS, Bank of England, HM Land Registry, Eurostat (some via FRED); fetch dates per series in `notes/household-panel.csv` and the table at the end. Latest CPI month: US 2026-09, UK 2026-09, euro area 2026-09. Nothing is synthetic except the fixed weekly shop of the work-time table, whose quantities are our own choice. Revised data (not real-time vintages)._

Index = 100 on the 2019 average. Changes are 12-month log changes in % unless marked. Real pay = pay ÷ CPI (US: hourly earnings of production and nonsupervisory workers ÷ CPI; UK: average weekly earnings ÷ CPI; euro area: the quarterly labour cost index of wages and salaries ÷ HICP).

## Main points

- **US:** real pay hit its 2021-24 low of 101.5 (2019 = 100) in 2022-06 and is 105.4 in 2026-09.
- **UK:** real pay hit its 2021-24 low of 99.6 (2019 = 100) in 2022-10 and is 105.7 in 2026-08.
- **Euro area:** real pay hit its 2021-24 low of 95.9 (2019 = 100) in 2023-06 and is 99.8 in 2026-06: still below its 2019 level.
- **UK income groups:** household costs rose 36.7% for the lowest-income tenth and 37.1% for the highest from 2021-01 to 2026-06 (ONS Household Costs Indices). The poorest were hit first: 14.5% against 11.3% in the year to 2022-10, as energy and food dominate their budgets. The richest caught up as mortgage rates rose: 1.7% against 4.0% in the year to 2024-06.
- **A gallon of US petrol** took 6.7 minutes of work in 2019, 11.0 at the 2022 peak and 8.2 in 2026-09.
- **The 2026 Hormuz closure:** for the UK, the chain's 82-points-lost scenario gives real pay -0.1% after 6 months (-3.0 to +1.9); it was -1.0%.
